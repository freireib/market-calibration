Methods & data.

What is scored?

Each row represents a specific binary event at a fixed time before the outcome. A Brier loss is (probability − outcome)², where outcome is 0 or 1. Only called, certified, or settled outcomes are eligible; an uncalled race is never scored.

Isochronic snapshots

The initial 2026 study uses September 3, October 3, and November 2 at 3:00 p.m. Eastern. These are two calendar months, one calendar month, and one day before the November 3 election. Quote timestamps must be at or before each snapshot.

Calibration bins and uncertainty

Forecasts are grouped in 0.10-wide probability bins. Each point is the (possibly weighted) mean forecast and observed win rate. Vertical intervals are approximate 95% Wilson intervals, using Kish effective sample size when weighted. Correlated election outcomes can make those intervals too narrow.

Volume

The primary analysis gives every race equal weight. Optional weights use contracts traded in the seven days before each snapshot, not lifetime or post-forecast activity. Market size and liquidity can affect inclusion; a volume-weighted result answers a different question than event-level calibration.

Missing data and comparability

Missing, stale, or invalid probabilities are excluded, not imputed. Turn on “Matched-race cohort only” to compare the same resolved races at all three horizons. Source coverage and gaps must be inspected before publishing conclusions.
